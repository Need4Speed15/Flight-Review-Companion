METHOD AND SCOPE
Read the primary PDF sections directly; answers were not derived from
third-party aircraft summaries. Text extraction was checked against page
renderings for the airspeed limits (PDF 48 / printed 2-4) and the dense
takeoff-performance table (PDF 134 / printed 5-14). In particular, OCR
misreads some 9s as I and the engine name as 10-360-L24; the cards use
the actual Lycoming IO-360-L2A designation and 9-knot headwind increment.

Each card names Cessna 172S, provides a specific printed section/page,
links to its primary PDF page, and carries the exact edition and review
date. Questions and explanations are original, concise study material.
They do not reproduce complete operating or emergency checklists.
The set covers configuration, fuel and oil, loading/CG, useful airspeed
limits, instrument/electrical systems, preflight and normal procedures,
recognition of selected emergencies, and applied performance planning.

IMPORTANT APPLICABILITY LIMIT
The 2004 revision is the edition inspected, not a claim that this is the
latest handbook or current approved information for every 172S. Individual
serial numbers, supplements, modifications, avionics installations, and
current airplane records can change applicability. Later 172S G1000 and
other 172 variants must not inherit the conventional-panel vacuum,
electrical, procedures, or equipment assumptions. The actual aircraft's
current approved POH/AFM, supplements, placards and weight/balance records
remain the operating authority. The publicly mirrored PDF is linked for
fact-checking; the PDF is not added to the site's tracked content.

NUMERIC AND APPLICATION CHECKS
- 180 hp / 2700 RPM; fixed-pitch propeller; static range 2300-2400 RPM.
- 56 gallons total / 53 usable; tabs 17.5 usable per tank.
- 5-quart minimum; 8 quarts for extended flight.
- Normal ramp 2558 lb, takeoff/landing 2550 lb; utility takeoff/landing
  2200 lb with rear seat unoccupied and baggage empty.
- Baggage limits 120 lb area 1, 50 lb area 2, 120 lb combined.
- At 2550 lb normal forward CG is 41.0 inches: the 39-inch example fails.
- VA 105/98/90 KIAS at 2550/2200/1900 lb. VFE 110 at 10 degrees,
  85 beyond 10 through 30 degrees. VNO 129; VNE 163 KIAS.
- Run-up at 1800 RPM: maximum magneto drop 150, differential 50 RPM.
  These differ from familiar limits on some other Cessna installations.
- Short-field takeoff 10 degrees / 56 KIAS over obstacle; slow retraction
  after 60 KIAS. Short-field landing 30 degrees / 61 KIAS until flare.
- The 68-KIAS restart speed is distinguished from 70-up/65-down KIAS
  immediate-after-takeoff and forced-landing checklist values.
- 15-knot demonstrated crosswind is expressly not a limitation.
- Takeoff chart: 2550 lb, 2000-foot pressure altitude, 30 C: 1285-foot
  ground roll / 2190 feet over 50 feet, subject to chart conditions.
- Wind correction is minus 10% per 9 knots headwind; plus 10% per 2
  knots tailwind up to 10 knots. Both rules are in note 3.
- Dry-grass correction adds 15% of the ground-roll figure to both
  distances; example 1000/1700 becomes 1150/1850 feet.
- Above-standard climb correction applies to time, fuel AND distance.
- The fuel-planning example's reserve is not described as a universal
  regulatory fuel minimum.

EXPANSION NUMERIC CHECKS
- Oil pressure must begin to show within 30 seconds in summer or about
  one minute in very cold weather; otherwise stop and investigate.
- Starter: 10 seconds cranking / 20 seconds cooling, three cycles, then
  10 minutes cooling. A further unsuccessful set calls for investigation.
- Ground leaning starts at 1200 RPM, then suitable idle/taxi RPM, normally
  800-1000; this is not the takeoff mixture setting.
- At or below 75% level-cruise power, recommended lean is 50 F rich of
  peak. This specific edition permits peak for best economy and expressly
  prohibits continuous lean-of-peak operation. This is NOT generalized to
  other engine approvals, modifications, or current aircraft handbooks.
- Landing with flaps up: approach +9 KIAS, distances +35%. Example:
  1455 x 1.35 = 1964.25 feet, rounded upward to approximately 1965.
- Dry-grass landing correction is 45% of ground roll, unlike the takeoff
  chart's 15%. With 650/1455-foot base figures, add 292.5 to both, giving
  942.5/1747.5 feet, rounded upward to 943/1748. No wet-grass approval or
  universal surface correction is implied.
- Standard-day climb 2000 to 8000 feet at 2550 lb: subtract cumulative
  entries, yielding 11 minutes, 2.2 gallons, 15 NM in zero wind. The
  separate start/taxi/takeoff allowance remains 1.4 gallons.
- Cruise 8000-foot pressure altitude / standard day / 2600 RPM / 2550 lb /
  recommended lean: 68% power, 119 KTAS, 9.4 GPH; two cruise hours uses
  18.8 gallons, excluding other phases and reserve.
- Alternate static, flaps up: 70 KIAS corresponds to 68 KCAS under the
  stated table conditions (windows/ventilators closed; heater, cabin air
  and defroster maximum).
- Power-off stall at 2550 lb, flaps up: about 48 KIAS wings level versus
  68 KIAS at 60-degree bank, for both CG extremes tabulated.
- With the integrated harness properly snug/low and retracted, at most
  one additional inch of lap belt should pull from the retractor.
